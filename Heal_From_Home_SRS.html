1. Introduction

1.1 Purpose

This Software Requirements Specification (SRS) establishes the complete, authoritative, and verifiable functional and non-functional requirements for "Heal From Home" (Release 1.0) — an intelligent, full-stack, AI-powered post-discharge recovery companion and remote patient monitoring platform.

The core scope encompassed by this specification spans the end-to-end post-hospitalization lifecycle: from the ingestion and automated parsing of clinical discharge summaries, down through personalized daily recovery roadmap generation, dynamic vital telemetry tracking, automated drug-drug and food-drug interaction screening, bi-directional doctor-patient triage communication, emergency 1-click SOS escalation, and continuous compassionate conversational guidance powered by large language models.

This document constitutes the technical baseline contract between the academic supervisors at Medi-Caps University, clinical domain advisors, quality assurance personnel, and the engineering development team. All architectural implementations, system validations, and formal acceptance tests shall be executed against the specifications defined herein.

1.2 Document Conventions

This document adheres rigorously to the IEEE Std 830-1998 Recommended Practice for Software Requirements Specifications. Throughout this document, the following typographical and semantic conventions are applied:

  • Requirement Identifiers: Every verifiable functional requirement is uniquely designated using the format REQ-XXX, where XXX represents an ascending 3-digit serial number grouped logically by system feature.
  • Priority Scale: Requirements are categorized using a 4-dimensional priority rating matrix:
    • Benefit: Value delivered to patient/clinician (Scale: 1 to 9).
    • Penalty: Adverse impact if the requirement is omitted (Scale: 1 to 9).
    • Cost: Development effort and architectural complexity (Scale: 1 to 9).
    • Risk: Implementation volatility or technological uncertainty (Scale: 1 to 9).
    • Categorical Priority: Derived classification assigned as HIGH (Mandatory for core clinical workflow), MEDIUM (Important clinical enhancement), or LOW (Desirable future enhancement).
  • Typography: Source code entities, endpoints, and database fields are styled in monospace. Key clinical terms and safety guardrails are marked in bold text.

1.4 Product Scope

Hospital discharge represents the most vulnerable transition in modern healthcare. Studies published in the New England Journal of Medicine and The Lancet document that nearly 19.6% of post-operative patients experience an adverse clinical event within 30 days of returning home, with over 60% of these incidents stemming from medication mismanagement, misunderstood discharge instructions, delayed recognition of physiological complications, or anxiety-induced isolation.

Heal From Home addresses this systemic void by acting as an always-accessible, intelligent digital bridge between the hospital ward and the patient's bedroom.

Primary Clinical & Operational Objectives:
  • Curtail 30-Day Preventable Readmissions: Lower post-surgical emergency department bounce-backs by 35% to 45% through continuous vital telemetry and early anomaly detection.
  • Eliminate Medical Jargon Confusion: Instantly convert complex hospital discharge summaries into plain-English, anxiety-reducing, step-by-step daily recovery protocols.
  • Ensure 100% Medication Adherence & Clinical Safety: Screen every prescribed drug against the patient's regimen for adverse drug-drug interactions and food contraindications (e.g., Statin & Grapefruit CYP3A4 toxicity).
  • Provide 1-Click Emergency Escalation: Empower distressed patients with a 1-tap 108 emergency ambulance dialer and automated SOS triage transmission to designated caregivers and doctors.

2. Overall Description

2.1 Product Perspective & Context

Heal From Home is a modern, self-contained, cloud-hosted Remote Patient Management System (RPMS) and clinical decision-support ecosystem. Unlike legacy hospital portals that serve merely as static PDF repositories, Heal From Home is dynamic, responsive, and bidirectional:

HEAL FROM HOME ARCHITECTURAL ECOSYSTEM:
• Patient Edge Layer: Responsive React 18 Single-Page Application (SPA) supporting desktop browsers, tablets, and smartphones, engineered with calming color psychology and accessible touch-first targets.
• Clinical API Gateway: High-throughput Node.js / Express micro-framework with JWT authentication, cryptographic password hashing, strict helmet security headers, and rate limiting.
• Intelligence Layer (MedAI): High-speed Groq LPU (Language Processing Unit) inference pipeline utilizing modern foundational clinical models (Qwen-2.5-32B / LLaMA-3.3-70B) for zero-latency medical reasoning and automated JSON extraction.
• Persistence & Telemetry Store: MongoDB Atlas clustered document database storing patient profiles, time-series health logs, multi-dose medication schedules, and clinical report analyses.

2.2 Product Functions (Core Capabilities)

The system executes eight primary functional subsystems:

  1. Secure Multi-Role Authentication: Granular Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) separating Patient, Doctor, and Administrative privilege levels with stateless JWT tokens.
  2. Automated Discharge Document OCR & Parsing: Intelligent document ingestion supporting multi-page PDF files and pasted summaries; parses clinical text into structured JSON entities without manual transcription.
  3. Personalized Recovery Pathway Engine: Dynamically generates phase-based recovery roadmaps (Acute, Subacute, Reconditioning) tailored to specific surgical procedures (Cardiac CABG/Stents, Orthopedic Arthroplasty, Abdominal Surgery).
  4. Time-Series Health Telemetry & Anomaly Alerts: Captures heart rate, blood pressure, oxygen saturation (SpO2), temperature, blood glucose, pain levels (0–10 scale), and mood; performs automated range-validation and triggers clinical alerts when vital signs cross critical safety thresholds.
  5. Automated Pharmacological Safety Checker: Analyzes prescribed regimens for severe drug-drug interactions, food conflicts, and provides vital-dependent hold instructions (e.g., hold beta-blockers if resting HR < 55 bpm).
  6. 1-Click Emergency SOS & Triage Dispatch: Telephony integration initiating direct 108/112 ambulance calls while simultaneously copying a structured emergency vital summary and alerting primary caregivers.
  7. Doctor Clinical Review & Patient Monitoring: High-density clinical view for attending physicians to inspect patient recovery scores, vital trend charts, report uploads, and triage urgency.
  8. MedAI Interactive 24/7 Care Assistant: Empathetic conversational clinical companion available around the clock to answer post-care questions, explain lab results, and provide compassionate recovery reassurance.

    • Host OS: Linux (Ubuntu 22.04 LTS / Debian 12 recommended) or Windows Server 2022.
  • Database Layer: MongoDB Atlas Cluster (v7.0+), supporting Mongoose Object-Document Mapping (ODM), replica-set high availability, and SSL/TLS encrypted connections.
  • AI Hardware Acceleration: Groq Cloud LPU (Language Processing Unit) infrastructure delivering sustained inference speeds exceeding 300 tokens/sec.

2.5 Design and Implementation Constraints

Key Architectural & Regulatory Constraints:
  • Clinical Safety Disclaimer Constraint: The software acts as an informational care companion and never provides definitive clinical diagnoses or replaces emergency hospital triage. Every AI response must mandate doctor consultation for acute symptoms.
  • Data Protection & Regulatory Compliance: Architecture must align with the principles of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDPA 2023) and HIPAA privacy rules regarding Protected Health Information (PHI). Passwords must be hashed with bcrypt (salt factor ≥ 10); API communications must enforce TLS 1.3.
  • Inference Latency Constraint: All AI-driven document analyses and chat responses must return within 3.5 seconds to prevent patient abandonment.
  • Stateless Micro-Services Architecture: Server nodes must remain completely stateless; session state is maintained entirely via signed JWT tokens to allow seamless horizontal scaling.

  • Hardware Independence: The system assumes manual or semi-automated vital entry; no mandatory proprietary hardware locks are enforced. Standard commercial pulse oximeters and BP cuffs are fully supported.
  • Third-Party Dependencies: Continuous operational availability of MongoDB Atlas cloud database, Groq Cloud LLM API, and standard Node Package Manager (npm) repositories.

3.3 Software Interfaces & API Services

  • Database Management System: MongoDB Atlas accessed via Mongoose 8.x ODM with connection pooling, automatic index optimization, and JSON schema validation.
  • AI Language Inference Engine: Groq Cloud Inference API communicating via official groq-sdk over secure HTTPS; models: qwen/qwen3.8-27b or llama-3.3-70b-versatile.
  • Document Parsing Engine: Server-side pdf-parse binary stream processor extracting raw textual layers from hospital discharge documents.
  • Data Visualization Engine: Chart.js and react-chartjs-2 rendering HTML5 canvas time-series curves with smooth Bezier splines and dynamic vital anomaly fills.

3.4 Communications Interfaces & Protocols

  • Transport Security: All client-to-server and server-to-cloud traffic mandates HTTPS over TLS 1.3 with strong cipher suites. Unencrypted HTTP is permanently blocked via HSTS.
  • Application Protocol: RESTful APIs adhering to OpenAPI 3.0 guidelines, consuming and emitting payloads exclusively formatted in application/json.
  • Telephony & SOS Dispatch: Native tel:108 and tel:112 protocol handler integration on mobile browsers for instantaneous emergency dispatch.

4. System Features (Functional Requirements)

4.1 Feature 1 – User Authentication & Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)

4.1.1 Description and Priority

Provides identity verification, account registration, credential management, and role-based permissions separating Patients, Doctors, and Administrators.

Priority: HIGH | Benefit: 9 | Penalty: 9 | Cost: 4 | Risk: 3

4.1.2 Stimulus / Response Sequences

1. User Action: Submits registration form with email, password, full name, and role ('patient' or 'doctor').
2. System Response: Validates email uniqueness, hashes password using bcrypt (salt factor 10), creates database record, and issues signed JWT token.
3. User Action: Submits login credentials.
4. System Response: Compares password hash; upon match, returns JWT token (7-day validity) and user profile payload; client stores token in local storage and redirects to respective role dashboard.
5. Error Condition: If credentials fail, returns HTTP 401 with structured message: {"success": false, "message": "Invalid email or password"}.

4.1.3 Detailed Functional Requirements

REQ ID Requirement Statement Priority
REQ-001 The system shall provide registration endpoints for both Patient and Doctor accounts with mandatory field validation. HIGH
REQ-002 The system shall enforce strong password complexity: minimum 8 characters including at least one digit and one special character. HIGH
REQ-003 All stored passwords shall be cryptographically salted and hashed using bcrypt with a work factor of at least 10 rounds. Plaintext passwords shall never be persisted or logged. HIGH
REQ-004 Upon successful authentication, the system shall generate a signed JSON Web Token (JWT) containing user ID, role, and a maximum expiration of 7 days. HIGH
REQ-005 The system shall validate the JWT on every protected route and enforce strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC); patients shall be prevented from accessing doctor or admin endpoints. HIGH
REQ-006 The system shall provide a /api/auth/me endpoint to re-hydrate client application state and verify token validity on browser refresh. HIGH
REQ-007 The system shall support secure password update workflows requiring the user's current password for verification. MEDIUM
REQ-008 The system shall terminate user sessions immediately upon client logout by clearing tokens from client storage and invalidating active state. HIGH
REQ-009 Failed login attempts shall be throttled using express-rate-limit to mitigate brute-force attacks (maximum 100 requests per 15 minutes per IP). HIGH
REQ-010 The system shall record a timestamp of the user's last login in the database for auditing and session tracking. LOW

5. Non-Functional Requirements

5.1 Performance Requirements

Metric / Parameter Performance Benchmark Specification Rationale & Impact
Page Initial Load Time Initial dashboard load and render shall complete within ≤ 2.5 seconds on a 10 Mbps broadband connection. Reduces cognitive strain on recovering patients experiencing fatigue.
API Endpoint Latency Standard CRUD REST endpoints (vitals, meds, auth) shall respond in ≤ 200 milliseconds. Ensures immediate UI responsiveness during data entry.
AI Inference Latency Document parsing and conversational LLM responses shall complete in ≤ 3.5 seconds. Maintains patient engagement during discharge document analysis.
Concurrent User Capacity The system architecture shall sustain a minimum of 250 concurrent active sessions without performance degradation. Supports multi-hospital cohort scaling on modest cloud hardware.
Database Query Performance Indexed telemetry queries (e.g., 7-day vital trends) shall execute in ≤ 50 milliseconds in MongoDB Atlas. Powers instantaneous rendering of multi-parameter Chart.js graphs.

5.2 Safety Requirements

  • Clinical Disclaimer Prominence: Every screen and AI output must explicitly inform the patient that Heal From Home is an assistive monitoring tool, not a diagnostic medical device.
  • Automated Emergency Triggering: Whenever vital metrics indicate acute decompensation (SpO2 < 90%, SBP > 200 mmHg, HR > 140 bpm), the system must immediately trigger visual red banners and highlight the 1-Click 108 ambulance dialer.
  • Vital-Dependent Medication Holds: The system must display clinical hold notices on sensitive drugs (e.g., beta-blockers, antihypertensives, insulin) when the patient's recorded vitals are incompatible with safe administration.
  • Fail-Safe Emergency Operation: In the event of backend network loss, client-side cached data must maintain the emergency contact phone numbers and local hospital addresses accessible at all times.

5.3 Security Requirements

  • Data Encryption in Transit: 100% of network communications shall enforce TLS 1.3 encryption with strict HTTPS redirection and HTTP Strict Transport Security (HSTS) headers.
  • Data Encryption at Rest: MongoDB Atlas database volumes shall utilize AES-256 encryption at rest. Sensitive patient identifiers shall be compartmentalized.
  • Password Storage: Passwords must be hashed using bcrypt with salt rounds ≥ 10. Passwords shall never appear in application logs or error stack traces.
  • Session Security & JWT: Authentication tokens must be signed with a cryptographically secure 256-bit secret key. Tokens expire automatically after 7 days and must be revoked upon explicit logout.
  • HTTP Security Headers: The Express application shall implement helmet middleware setting Content Security Policy (CSP), X-Frame-Options: DENY, and X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff.
  • Rate Limiting: Public authentication endpoints shall enforce rate limits of 100 requests per 15-minute window per IP to eliminate brute-force attack vectors.

5.4 Software Quality Attributes

Quality Attribute Quantitative Quality Specification
Availability The system shall maintain 99.9% operational uptime (< 43 minutes unplanned downtime per month), excluding scheduled maintenance windows.
Reliability & Fault Tolerance All database transactions modifying patient health state shall be atomic. If third-party AI services become unreachable, the core vitals and medication logging systems shall continue operating uninterrupted without throwing fatal exceptions.
Usability & Accessibility The UI shall comply with WCAG 2.1 Level AA standards. A newly registered post-operative patient shall be able to complete their morning vital sign logging and medication check-off within under 3 minutes without requiring assistance.
Maintainability & Modularity Source code shall adhere to ESLint Airbnb standards for React and PEP 8 for Python utilities. Controllers, routes, and middleware shall remain decoupled with modular separation of concerns.
Portability The backend application shall run cleanly across any standard Node.js runtime environment (Linux, macOS, Windows Server) and shall be containerizable via standard Dockerfiles without environment-specific code alterations.

7. Appendix A: Comprehensive Glossary

Term / Acronym Medical & Technical Definition
AI / LLM Artificial Intelligence / Large Language Model. Foundational deep learning neural networks trained on vast textual datasets capable of natural language understanding and clinical reasoning.
BP Blood Pressure. Measure of hydrostatic pressure exerted by circulating blood upon blood vessel walls; recorded as Systolic over Diastolic in mmHg.
CABG Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting. Major cardiovascular surgical procedure diverting blood around clogged coronary arteries to restore myocardial perfusion.
CYP3A4 Cytochrome P450 3A4. Crucial liver enzyme involved in the metabolism of approximately 50% of pharmaceutical medications, including statins. Strongly inhibited by compounds in grapefruit juice.
DAPT Dual Antiplatelet Therapy. Mandatory post-stenting pharmacological protocol combining Aspirin with a P2Y12 inhibitor (e.g., Clopidogrel) to prevent fatal stent thrombosis.
DES Drug-Eluting Stent. Coronary endovascular scaffold coated with an antiproliferative medication (e.g., Zotarolimus, Everolimus) that slowly elutes to prevent neointimal hyperplasia and restenosis.
EBD Evidence-Based Design. Architectural and graphical design methodology leveraging clinical psychology research to foster calming, restorative environments that reduce patient anxiety.
EHR / EMR Electronic Health Record / Electronic Medical Record. Digital version of a patient's comprehensive medical history, operative notes, and hospital discharge summaries.
Groq LPU Language Processing Unit. Specialized custom silicon hardware accelerator engineered by Groq specifically for high-throughput, ultra-low latency large language model inference.
HIPAA Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996. United States federal statutory standard establishing strict safeguards for protecting sensitive patient health information.
JWT JSON Web Token. An open standard (RFC 7519) compact, URL-safe means of securely transmitting claims between parties as a digitally signed JSON object.
LVEF Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction. Echocardiographic measurement of the percentage of blood pumped out of the heart's main pumping chamber with each contraction (Normal: 50%–70%).
MVT / MVC Model-View-Template / Model-View-Controller. Software design patterns enforcing strict separation between data persistence (Model), business logic (Controller/View), and graphical rendering (Template/View).
NSTEMI Non-ST Elevation Myocardial Infarction. Type of acute coronary syndrome characterized by partial coronary artery occlusion resulting in myocardial ischemia and biomarker elevation without ST-elevation on ECG.
PCI / PTCA Percutaneous Coronary Intervention / Percutaneous Transluminal Coronary Angioplasty. Minimally invasive catheter-based procedure used to open clogged heart arteries using balloons and stents.
PHI Protected Health Information. Individually identifiable health information created, stored, or transmitted by a healthcare entity regarding physical or mental health conditions or treatments.
RBAC Role-Based Access Control. Security architecture mechanism restricting system access authorized to specific users based on their defined organizational roles (Patient, Doctor, Administrator).
RPMS Remote Patient Management System. Digital healthcare platform enabling continuous or periodic monitoring of recovering patients outside conventional clinical settings.
SpO2 Peripheral Capillary Oxygen Saturation. Estimation of the oxygen saturation level in arterial blood expressed as a percentage, non-invasively measured via pulse oximetry (Normal: 95%–100%).
WCAG Web Content Accessibility Guidelines. Globally recognized technical standards published by the W3C defining criteria for making web content accessible to individuals with visual, cognitive, or physical impairments.
